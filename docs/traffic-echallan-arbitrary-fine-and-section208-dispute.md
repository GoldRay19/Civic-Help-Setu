# Traffic E-Challan Arbitrary Fine, Defective Electronic Camera & Section 208 Dispute Workspace

## Overview & Statutory Mandate

The **Traffic E-Challan Dispute Workspace** equips vehicle owners, transport operators, and citizens with an evidence-grade calculation and legal petition drafting framework under the **Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (as amended by the Motor Vehicles Amendment Act, 2019)** and **Rule 167A of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989 (CMVR)**.

With the proliferation of automated electronic enforcement systems (ANPR cameras, speed radar traps, and red-light violation cameras), vehicle owners frequently receive arbitrary e-challans generated by malfunctioning optical character recognition (OCR), speed guns without valid annual calibration certificates under the **Legal Metrology Act, 2009**, or notices dispatched weeks after the statutory notification window.

This workspace operationalizes the evidentiary requirements of **Rule 167A** and statutory rights under **Section 208** into an offline-first dispute workflow.

## Statutory Framework & Key Provisions

### 1. Rule 167A Electronic Monitoring Standards (CMVR)
Under the Central Motor Vehicles (Seventeenth Amendment) Rules, 2021:
- **Mandatory Visual Clarity:** The electronic monitoring system must record a clear, high-resolution photograph or video showing the vehicle's registration plate, overall make/model, and exact road demarcations (Rule 167A(2)).
- **Legal Metrology Annual Calibration:** Speed cameras, radar guns, and lidar speed measurement devices must possess a valid annual calibration certificate from an accredited legal metrology laboratory. Uncertified speed measurements are inadmissible in court.
- **Statutory 15-Day Service Rule (Rule 167A(4)):** The notice of offence must be sent electronically within **15 days** of the date of occurrence, preserving the citizen's opportunity to secure dashcam and alibi records.

### 2. Common Evidentiary Infirmities & Grounds of Challenge
| Defect Type | Statutory Ground | Legal Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Uncalibrated Speed Camera** | Rule 167A(2) & Legal Metrology Act, 2009 | **Fatal defect:** Speed recording legally uncertified and inadmissible. |
| **Vehicle Class Mismatch** | Section 194B / Section 194D MV Act | **Fatal defect:** e.g., Helmet challan levied on a 4-wheeler due to OCR error. |
| **Duplicate Challans** | Article 20(2) Constitution of India | **Void:** Multiple captures within minutes along the same uninterrupted road corridor. |
| **Cloned Number Plate / Alibi** | Section 11 of Indian Evidence Act | **Exculpatory:** Fastag toll plaza logs or parking receipts proving vehicle presence elsewhere. |
| **Emergency Vehicle Obstruction** | Section 194E of MV Act, 1988 | **Immunity:** Crossing stop line solely to give way to an ambulance or fire tender. |
| **Obscured Signage** | Section 116 MV Act & IRC:67 Standards | **Vulnerable:** Lack of statutory notice of speed limit due to obscured or missing signboards. |
| **Delayed Notice (>15 Days)** | Rule 167A(4) CMVR | **Procedural Invalidity:** Deprives vehicle owner of contemporaneous defense. |

### 3. Procedural Pathways under Section 208
- **Virtual Court (vcourts.gov.in):** For summary disposal under Section 208. The citizen may elect **"Contest / Trial in Court"** rather than paying the compounding fee.
- **Traffic Police Nodal Grievance Cell:** Administrative representation requesting cancellation before the challan is transmitted to the court database.

### Data Storage & Privacy Protection
1. **Local Vault Key:** `civicbridge_echallan_dispute_vault`
2. **Settings Key:** `civicbridge_echallan_settings`
3. **SHA-256 Tamper-Evident Seal:** Each dispute case computes a canonical cryptographic digest over key fields (vehicle plate, challan number, date, amount, selected defects, timestamps) to verify evidentiary integrity.
4. **DPDP Act 2023 Compliance:** Vehicle registration numbers and contact details are masked upon export (`KA-01-XX-4099`, `98XXXXXX45`, `c***@parivahan.gov`).
